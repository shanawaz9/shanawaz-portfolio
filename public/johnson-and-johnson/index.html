01

The beginning

I joined as the only designer on a Governance, Risk & Compliance (GRC) platform for managing who has access to what. Its users were compliance teams, auditors, and business teams.

The product was powerful but hard to use. Pages were crowded with data, tasks took too many steps, and screens looked and worked differently from each other.

My goal was not to remove the detail, since auditors need it. It was to make that detail easy to move through, so users always know their next step.

02

Growing the product and the team

Release by release, the product grew. We added compliance flows, delegation, approver flows, dashboards, and a conversational AI assistant.

The team grew too. I moved from solo designer to Senior UX Designer and helped hire 6 designers. I set up the design system and the way we worked, so the whole team designed to one standard.

03

The problems and how I solved them

Five problems shaped most of the work.

  1. Pages with too much dataUsers had to scan crowded pages to find what mattered.
  2. Workflows with too many stepsKey tasks like compliance, delegation, and approvals took too many steps.
  3. Answers spread across dashboardsA simple question meant clicking through several dashboards or asking the compliance team.
  4. Screens that didn't matchSimilar screens looked and worked differently.
  5. Keeping quality at release speedWith frequent releases, details can get lost between design and code.

How I solved them

  • User research. Talked to users and stakeholders to learn how compliance work really happens.
  • Defining the problem. Turned business requirements into clear user problems.
  • User journeys. Used user journeys and storyboards to find where people got stuck.
  • Crazy 8s rapid ideation. Sketched many ideas quickly with Crazy 8s before picking one.
  • Stakeholder collaboration. I worked closely with product owners and business stakeholders to keep every change within the agreed requirements. When a bigger design change would clearly help users, I made the case using user journeys and data, and we agreed on it together.
  • Design reviews. Checked designs with product owners, business analysts, and AI engineers.
  • Developer handoff. Shared clear specs and components. My technical background helped the build match the design.
04

The result

The product serves 600,000+ users and holds a 4.2 out of 5 rating from its in-app feedback tool.

  • Users find answers and finish tasks faster.
  • The business gets fewer routine requests to compliance teams.
  • The client got a consistent, on-brand product across 13+ releases.
  • The company gained 6 new designers, a design system, and an AI pattern it can reuse.
·

What I learned

Good design doesn't make compliance simple. It makes it easy to find your way through.

As a product designer, I learned that big product changes rest on strong basics: a design system, a clear process, and a team working to one standard. That is what let us bring AI into the product without losing users' trust.
