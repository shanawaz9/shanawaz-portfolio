Part 01

The GRC platform

01

The beginning

I joined as the only designer on a Governance, Risk & Compliance (GRC) platform for managing who has access to what. Its users were compliance teams, auditors, and business teams.

The product was powerful but hard to use.

My goal was not to remove the detail, since auditors need it. It was to make that detail easy to move through, so users always know their next step.

02

Growing the product and the team

Release by release, the product grew. We added compliance flows, delegation, approver flows, dashboards, and a conversational AI assistant.

The team grew too. I moved from solo designer to Senior UX Designer and helped hire 6 designers. I set up the design system and the way we worked, so the whole team designed to one standard.

03

The problems and how I solved them

Five problems shaped most of the work.

  1. Pages with too much dataUsers had to scan crowded pages to find what mattered.
  2. Workflows with too many stepsKey tasks like compliance, delegation, and approvals took too many steps.
  3. Answers spread across dashboardsA simple question meant clicking through several dashboards or asking the compliance team.
  4. Screens that didn't matchSimilar screens looked and worked differently.
  5. Keeping quality at release speedWith frequent releases, details can get lost between design and code.

How I solved them

  • User research. Talked to users and stakeholders to learn how compliance work really happens.
  • Defining the problem. Turned business requirements into clear user problems.
  • User journeys. Used user journeys and storyboards to find where people got stuck.
  • Crazy 8s rapid ideation. Sketched many ideas quickly with Crazy 8s before picking one.
  • Stakeholder collaboration. I worked closely with product owners and business stakeholders to keep every change within the agreed requirements. When a bigger design change would clearly help users, I made the case using user journeys and data, and we agreed on it together.
  • Design reviews. Checked designs with product owners, business analysts, and AI engineers.
  • Developer handoff. Shared clear specs and components. My technical background helped the build match the design.
04

From 12–14 steps to about six

Completing certain tasks required users to go through around 12–14 steps. They had to navigate multiple screens and rely on guidance to understand the process.

The challenge was to reduce this complexity and make the platform easier to use without adding more complexity to the experience.

The redesigned workflow reduced the number of steps required for the relevant task from around 12–14 to approximately six.

Part 02

The AI assistant

05

Exploring conversational AI

We explored how conversational AI could simplify the platform's workflows and help users complete tasks more easily.

My role

I worked as the sole designer on this project. I was responsible for understanding the problem, researching user needs, exploring conversational UX patterns, and designing the assistant experience.

I worked closely with product managers, product owners, AI engineers, and developers throughout the process.

Research

I started by conducting workshops with stakeholders, reviewing analytics and user pain points, and mapping user journeys and personas.

06

An assistant that could only guide

We started with a guidance-focused assistant that helped users understand the process and find the information they needed.

However, it could guide users but could not perform actions for them.

I felt we could go further by making the assistant more useful and allowing users to complete tasks through conversation.

07

Making the case for real conversation

One of the challenges was deciding how users should interact with the assistant.

When I started designing the conversational experience, the product owners were going in a different direction. They wanted UI buttons and components, with the AI in control.

The AI would lead with pre-made questions and follow-ups. The conversation stayed in the AI's control, and it didn't feel like conversational UX.

So I went back to research. I studied conversational UX research papers, recognised the common conversational UX patterns, and ran a competitive analysis.

I turned this into a presentation for the product owners, explaining the existing conversational UX patterns.

I showed why the old way would not solve the existing user problems: it would become UI again. We needed a solution that solved the actual problem.

08

Conversation first, UI when needed

I based the assistant on the Natural Conversation Framework (NCF) from Conversational UX Design by Robert J. Moore and Raphael Arar. It combines conversation analysis, the study of how people really talk, with UX design.

Its core idea is conversation first: if it works as a natural conversation, it works anywhere.

NCF breaks a conversation into three layers of reusable patterns:

Activity
What the conversation is aboutThe user asks a question or makes a request.
Sequence
Keeping each exchange on trackRepair: the assistant checks what the user meant, and the user can correct themselves.
Conversation
Opening and closing the chatA clear greeting, and a clean close once the task is done.

Instead of filling the chat with UI elements, I proposed using contextual components such as chips, dropdowns, and buttons only when needed.

09

From guidance to action

The assistant evolved from providing guidance to supporting actions through conversation.

Users could now raise access requests directly through the assistant, completing the task right in the conversation.

The goal was to make the experience more conversational while keeping structured UI elements where they helped users provide information and make selections.

Clarity and trust

Users need to trust what the assistant tells them, so every answer comes with a way to check it.

Every answer
Relevant resources alongsideThe AI understands the question and provides the relevant resources with each answer.
Part 03

The outcome

10

The result

The product serves 200,000+ users. Overall, we received positive business feedback, with an average reported rating of 4/5.

  • Users find answers and finish tasks faster.
  • The business gets fewer routine requests to compliance teams.
  • The client got a consistent, on-brand product across 13+ releases.
  • The company gained 6 new designers, a design system, and an AI pattern it can reuse.
·

What I learned

Good design doesn't make compliance simple. It makes it easy to find your way through.

This project helped me understand how conversational AI can simplify complex enterprise workflows. I learned that a good conversational experience is not just about adding a chatbot, but about helping users complete tasks more naturally.

It also reinforced the importance of balancing natural conversation, contextual UI, technical feasibility, and user control.

As a product designer, I learned that big product changes rest on strong basics: a design system, a clear process, and a team working to one standard. That is what let us bring AI into the product without losing users' trust.
