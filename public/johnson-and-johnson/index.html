01

Overview

I worked on an enterprise-grade GRC (Governance, Risk, and Compliance) platform focused on Identity & Access Management, and an AI-powered conversational assistant designed to help users query complex GRC information in plain language.

The product was used internally by compliance teams, auditors, and business stakeholders operating in a highly regulated environment, where every workflow, approval, and data point carries real governance weight.

02

Role & responsibilities

The platform evolved continuously across more than a dozen releases, so the work wasn't a single redesign: it was maintaining design integrity over a long-running, high-stakes product.

  • Simplified complex, multi-step user flows, significantly reducing task completion time.
  • Redesigned the UI across the application, aligning it with Material UI and industry standards.
  • Built and maintained a design system to ensure reusability, consistency, and scalability.
  • Supported 13+ product releases, ensuring design continuity across iterations.
  • Designed new compliance and approval workflows aligned with enterprise governance rules.
  • Improved data analytics interfaces to make insights clearer and more actionable.
  • Ensured pixel-perfect UI handoff by closely collaborating with developers.
  • Aligned the entire product experience with GRC-specific branding guidelines.
03

Process & design decisions

Conversational AI for GRC

A key part of my work involved designing an AI assistant embedded within the GRC platform: one built to act as a support layer, not a shortcut around understanding. I functioned as the design bridge between compliance complexity and usable interfaces, balancing technical feasibility against usability.

  1. Ask in plain language: a GRC question typed the way a person would actually ask it.
  2. Interpret intent: conversational UX patterns tuned for compliance-specific queries.
  3. Retrieve GRC data: pulled from the underlying compliance and access data, with no manual dashboard hunting.
  4. Support the decision: the response is framed to help the user decide, not to act on their behalf.

Contributions here included exploring natural language interaction models suited for enterprise and compliance contexts, designing AI responses that supported decision-making rather than automating it away, and improving overall UI cleanliness so AI-led interactions didn't feel bolted onto an existing dashboard.

Guiding principle: focus on conversational UX instead of reverting to clickable UI actions: the assistant helps users understand, rather than acting on their behalf.

04

Collaboration

I worked closely with AI engineers, full-stack developers, business analysts, QA, and product managers: aligning conversational logic with what the interface could realistically surface, keeping handoff pixel-perfect across a fast release cadence, and balancing enterprise governance rules against day-to-day usability.

05

Outcomes & impact

Exact metrics are confidential, but the direction of impact was consistent across releases:

  • Faster access to GRC information, without navigating multiple dashboards.
  • Reduced reliance on manual compliance teams for routine queries.
  • Increased confidence in AI-assisted workflows.
  • A reusable Conversational UX framework applicable across other GRC domains.
·

Key takeaway

Good UX doesn't simplify compliance: it makes it navigable.

Designing for enterprise GRC systems reinforced that the job isn't to hide complexity: auditors and compliance teams need the rigor. It's to give that complexity a shape people can move through without getting lost.
