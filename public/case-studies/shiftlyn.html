01 — The Problem

AI is changing the job market
faster than people can adapt.

AI is changing the job market faster than ever. While new skills are becoming essential, many professionals are still not learning or adapting to them.

We saw an opportunity to help people understand:

  • How AI impacts their career
  • Where their skill gaps exist
  • What they should learn next
  • How they can transition into AI-driven roles

Can we build a product — Shiftlyn — that helps people understand their AI readiness and guides them toward the right upskilling path?

Building the team

We kept the team lean, but made cross-collaboration the default rather than the exception. Developers, designers, and QA sat in the same conversations from day one instead of handing work off in stages.

That closeness meant design decisions got stress-tested against technical constraints immediately, and QA caught usability gaps before they ever reached real users — shortening the distance between an idea and a validated feature.

06 — Discovering the Core Product

The results page
is the product.

The real value was in the results page. The assessment simply collected data — but the results page is where the user actually experiences Shiftlyn.

The results page delivered:

  • AI readiness insights
  • Skill gap analysis
  • Learning recommendations
  • Career guidance

Designing for emotion

This is also the moment users are most emotionally invested — anxious about what the score will say, and looking for reassurance as much as information. So the results page couldn't just present data; it had to be designed around how someone feels reading it.

We thought about the experience as an emotional arc: Anxiety → Understanding → Acceptance → Action. The score comes first, but it's immediately followed by context — why this score, what it means, and what to do next — so users never sit alone with just a number.

Tone, pacing, and visual hierarchy were all designed around this arc. Reassuring language, a calm color system, and a clear sense of "next step" turned what could feel like a verdict into something that felt like guidance.

This became the core of the product experience.

07 — Scaling the Product

Initially, we targeted a 4% conversion rate. As we progressed, we realized our value proposition wasn't strong enough to reach that target.

Users needed:

  • More actionable insights
  • Better recommendations
  • Greater personalization

The solution wasn't better marketing. The solution was creating more value.

2. Choosing the right login pattern

Login and onboarding aren't one-size-fits-all — what works for a social app fails for a one-time assessment tool. We initially gated the report behind a standard email OTP, the default most teams reach for. It was the wrong call.

Not every product needs a full account-creation flow before it can deliver value. Our users had already paid — they didn't need to prove their identity again through a fragile email round-trip. The more friction we added between payment and the report, the more paying users we risked losing at the very last step.

We compared the realistic options for an Indian audience on one question: what's most likely to fail between "user pays" and "user sees their report"?

Login method How it works Drop-off risk Reliability Fits the post-payment gate?
Email OTPcurrent Code emailed, user types it High — every slow, spam, or dropped email is a stuck paying user Weak — email deliverability is the #1 silent failure ✗ Worst fit as a gate — fine only as a return-login option
Magic link Click a link in an email to log in High — same email dependency as OTP Weak, same as above ✗ Same flaw — don't gate the report on it
Phone / SMS OTP Code sent via SMS to phone Low–medium — SMS lands reliably in India; cost per message Strong in India, much better than email ◐ Good for return logins — not as the buy-gate
WhatsApp OTP Code via WhatsApp Business API Low — Indians live on WhatsApp, near-instant Very strong in India ◐ Great fallback channel — needs WhatsApp Business setup
Continue with Google / Apple OAuth — no code sent at all Very low — one tap, nothing to deliver Strongest — no email or SMS to fail ✓ Excellent — kills the deliverability problem entirely
Password User sets and types a password Medium — friction plus reset-flow pain Strong — no delivery dependency ◐ Fine as fallback credential — not primary
Order-token access The payment success page itself unlocks the report using the order ID Lowest — they're already on the page Strongest — independent of any message ✓✓ Best immediate unlock — this is the real fix
Login Method Comparison — India, Post-Payment Context
The fix

The payment success page now unlocks the report directly with the order ID — zero deliverability risk, because nothing has to be sent anywhere. Google/Apple sign-in became the primary path for return visits, with phone and WhatsApp OTP kept as fallbacks for an audience that trusts SMS and WhatsApp far more than email. Email OTP and magic links were demoted from gate to optional.

16 — Designing for Trust and Conversion

Marketing landing page:
trust before action.

AI career displacement is an emotionally charged topic. Users arrive with uncertainty about how AI might affect their future. The challenge wasn't just getting users to start the assessment — it was building enough trust for them to believe the results and take action.

To solve this, I designed the landing page using the C-TRUST-A Framework, a structure that guides users from awareness to action by progressively building clarity, credibility, and confidence.

shiftlyn.com
  • C
    Clarity

    One headline, one question, one next step — no ambiguity about what this product does.

  • T
    Transparency

    Time, cost, and data use are stated upfront — "2 minutes, no signup."

  • R
    Reliability

    Citing WEF and McKinsey research signals this isn't guesswork.

  • U
    Understandability

    A simple three-step structure replaces jargon with plain language.

  • S
    Social proof

    Real quotes from real roles, not generic praise.

  • T
    Trust

    An FAQ answers the hesitations users haven't said out loud yet.

  • A
    Action

    One final, low-friction CTA — the only decision left to make.

17 — Conclusion

Clarity is the product,
not the content.

What started as a simple AI readiness assessment evolved into Shiftlyn — a much broader product vision.

The MVP helped validate the problem, uncover user needs, identify usability challenges, refine the target audience, and shape a stronger product strategy. It also highlighted critical areas such as personalization, retention, pricing, scalability, and ongoing engagement.

One of the most important realizations was that users aren't shopping for another learning platform — they already have more content than they can use. What they lack is clarity: where they stand today, what's actually at risk, and which specific action moves them forward next.

The assessment became the entry point, but the real value lived in the insights, recommendations, and continuous guidance that followed.

Great products don't just provide information. They help people make better decisions.

And in a world where AI is reshaping careers every day, helping people make those decisions with confidence is the problem worth solving.
