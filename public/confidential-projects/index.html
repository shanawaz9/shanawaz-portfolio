Enterprise SaaS, Healthcare & AI Systems

Two confidential product design engagements — a conversational AI layer built into an enterprise GRC platform, and a ground-up UX overhaul of a legacy healthcare & e-commerce system.

A note on confidentiality. Both projects were built under NDA for enterprise clients. Screenshots, exact metrics, and internal product details have been intentionally left out — what follows describes the problem, process, and role, not the proprietary product itself.

Project 01

Conversational AI & GRC Platform (IAM)

ClientJohnson & Johnson
RoleSenior Product Designer
Releases13+ shipped
FocusIdentity & Access Management, Conversational AI
01

Overview

I worked on an enterprise-grade GRC (Governance, Risk, and Compliance) platform focused on Identity & Access Management, and an AI-powered conversational assistant designed to help users query complex GRC information in plain language.

The product was used internally by compliance teams, auditors, and business stakeholders operating in a highly regulated environment — where every workflow, approval, and data point carries real governance weight.

02

Role & responsibilities

The platform evolved continuously across more than a dozen releases, so the work wasn't a single redesign — it was maintaining design integrity over a long-running, high-stakes product.

  • Simplified complex, multi-step user flows, significantly reducing task completion time.
  • Redesigned the UI across the application, aligning it with Material UI and industry standards.
  • Built and maintained a design system to ensure reusability, consistency, and scalability.
  • Supported 13+ product releases, ensuring design continuity across iterations.
  • Designed new compliance and approval workflows aligned with enterprise governance rules.
  • Improved data analytics interfaces to make insights clearer and more actionable.
  • Ensured pixel-perfect UI handoff by closely collaborating with developers.
  • Aligned the entire product experience with GRC-specific branding guidelines.
03

Process & design decisions

Conversational AI for GRC

A key part of my work involved designing an AI assistant embedded within the GRC platform — one built to act as a support layer, not a shortcut around understanding. I functioned as the design bridge between compliance complexity and usable interfaces, balancing technical feasibility against usability.

  1. Ask in plain language — a GRC question typed the way a person would actually ask it.
  2. Interpret intent — conversational UX patterns tuned for compliance-specific queries.
  3. Retrieve GRC data — pulled from the underlying compliance and access data, with no manual dashboard hunting.
  4. Support the decision — the response is framed to help the user decide, not to act on their behalf.

Contributions here included exploring natural language interaction models suited for enterprise and compliance contexts, designing AI responses that supported decision-making rather than automating it away, and improving overall UI cleanliness so AI-led interactions didn't feel bolted onto an existing dashboard.

Guiding principle: focus on conversational UX instead of reverting to clickable UI actions — the assistant helps users understand, rather than acting on their behalf.
04

Collaboration

I worked closely with AI engineers, full-stack developers, business analysts, QA, and product managers — aligning conversational logic with what the interface could realistically surface, keeping handoff pixel-perfect across a fast release cadence, and balancing enterprise governance rules against day-to-day usability.

05

Outcomes & impact

Exact metrics are confidential, but the direction of impact was consistent across releases:

  • Faster access to GRC information, without navigating multiple dashboards.
  • Reduced reliance on manual compliance teams for routine queries.
  • Increased confidence in AI-assisted workflows.
  • A reusable Conversational UX framework applicable across other GRC domains.
—

Key takeaway

Good UX doesn't simplify compliance — it makes it navigable.

Designing for enterprise GRC systems reinforced that the job isn't to hide complexity — auditors and compliance teams need the rigor. It's to give that complexity a shape people can move through without getting lost.

Project 02

End-to-End Healthcare Website & E-commerce Platform

ClientLiberty Dental Plan ↗
RoleLead UX Designer
System age10–15 years, legacy
FocusInformation architecture, accessibility, e-commerce
Explore the live platform This case study covers UX work delivered for Liberty Dental Plan's public website and e-commerce experience.
Visit libertydentalplan.com
01

Overview

I led the UX design for a public-facing healthcare website and e-commerce platform built on a 10–15 year old legacy system with massive data volume and highly fragmented navigation.

The platform served diverse user groups and had grown complex over time, making usability, discoverability, and conversions a major challenge.

02

Leadership & role

As Lead UX Designer, the role extended beyond producing screens into owning the strategy across a large, data-heavy healthcare ecosystem.

  • Led the end-to-end UX effort for the website and e-commerce platform.
  • Owned UX strategy while coordinating with business, product, and stakeholders.
  • Managed complexity across a large, data-heavy healthcare ecosystem.
  • Collaborated with designers having 10+ years of UX experience.
  • Acted as the primary UX point of contact throughout the project lifecycle.
03

Research & discovery

Before redesigning anything, the priority was understanding where the existing experience was actually failing people.

  • Conducted UX research to understand existing pain points.
  • Ran stakeholder interviews to align business and user needs.
  • Analyzed existing user behavior using analytics and performance data.
04

Information architecture

A decade of incremental additions had left the content fragmented — the fix had to happen in the structure, not just the surface. Navigation chaos was solved through:

  • Card sorting to categorize large volumes of content.
  • Information architecture to organize and prioritize data.
  • Site mapping to simplify journeys across the platform.
The IA wasn't finalized in isolation — it was iterated collaboratively with stakeholders and product teams until the structure held up against real business priorities.
05

Wireframing & validation

Wireframes gave the new structure something to react to — and every shape was tested rather than assumed.

  • Created wireframes for ideation and structure.
  • Conducted A/B testing on key flows.
  • Facilitated multiple review sessions with the client.
  • Applied heuristic evaluation, UX principles, existing healthcare UI patterns, and data-driven decision-making.
06

Visual & UI design

UI design aligned with brand guidelines, but accessibility carried the most weight — a healthcare product can't afford to be unreadable or unclear to any of its users.

Accessibility best practices were applied with guidance from senior UX designers, ensuring consistency, clarity, and readability across the platform.

07

E-commerce design — the plan purchase flow

I designed the plan purchase flow end to end — optimizing the conversion path to make buying a plan simpler, clearer, and less cognitively demanding, while still meeting compliance requirements.

Before

A compliance-heavy checkout, built around what legal and financial teams needed to capture rather than what a buyer needed to understand.

After

A guided, plain-language purchase flow — compliance requirements still met, but surfaced in a way that didn't overwhelm the buyer.

08

Challenges & ownership

This project demanded strategic thinking, leadership, and patience — not just visual craft.

  • Navigating a large, decade-plus-old legacy codebase.
  • Managing overwhelming amounts of content and data without losing clarity.
  • Aligning multiple stakeholders who didn't always want the same thing.
  • Leading UX decisions under deadlines, legacy limits, and business pressure.
—

Key takeaway

Leading this project strengthened the ability to handle large-scale, legacy healthcare systems; drive UX clarity in data-heavy environments; lead cross-functional collaboration under pressure; and balance accessibility, compliance, and conversion — often within the same screen.
