255: <div class="t"><section class="ch" id="s1">
256:   <div class="ch-num">01 — The Idea</div>
257:   <h2>Everyone was scared.<br>Nobody had an answer.</h2>
258:   <p>Every tool trying to answer "will AI take my job?" gave the same vague paragraph. No numbers. Nothing role-specific. The answers were too general to be useful.</p>
259:   <p>I wanted something that gave you a number. Your role, your tasks, your actual risk. That was the gap.</p>
260:   <div class="pq"><p>People don't need another think-piece about AI. They need something specific to them.</p></div>
261:   <h3>The market wasn't hypothetical</h3>
262:   <p>Before writing a line of code, needed to know if the problem was measurable.</p>
263: </section></div>
264: 
265: <div class="w">
266: <div class="d4">
267:   <div class="dc"><div class="dn">$10.4B</div><div class="dd">India edtech market in 2025</div><div class="ds">→ $30B by 2030 · 37M paying users</div></div>
268:   <div class="dc"><div class="dn">52%</div><div class="dd">of 18–24 yr olds worried about AI impact on careers</div></div>
269:   <div class="dc"><div class="dn">83%</div><div class="dd">of Indian workers willing to delegate work to AI</div><div class="ds">Anxious, but open to using AI</div></div>
270:   <div class="dc"><div class="dn">22k+</div><div class="dd">employees hit by AI layoffs in early 2026 alone</div></div>
271: </div>
272: </div>
273: 
274: <div class="t">
275:   <p>Peer-reviewed research shows AI anxiety increases willingness to pay for knowledge products. The lower someone's confidence, the higher the payment intent. So the audience was real, and the demand was already there.</p>
276:   <h3>The competitive gap</h3>
277:   <p>Found 8+ global calculators. All free. All US/UK focused — USD pricing, Western jobs, zero LPA context. None built specifically for Indian working professionals. That gap was wide open.</p>
278: </div>

311: <div class="t"><section class="ch" id="s3">
312:   <div class="ch-num">03 — Breaking Into Pages</div>
313:   <h2>One page was doing<br>too many jobs.</h2>
314:   <p>Single-page version: quiz and results fighting for the same space. No progression. Split into three — /, /quiz, /results. Forced us to answer questions we'd been avoiding: what does each page actually do on its own?</p>
315:   <h3>The quiz</h3>
316:   <p>Five questions. Progress bar, input-validation microcopy, shake-animation instead of red error text — red errors can feel harsh. Not the right tone for a product built around job anxiety.</p>
317: </section></div>
318: 
319: <div class="w">
320: <div class="ip">
321:   <div>
323:     <div class="ic"><span class="il">Step 1 · Personalised entry</span><span class="id">The opening screen branches by who you are — working professional or student — so the rest of the quiz can ask the right questions.</span></div>
324:   </div>
325:   <div>
327:     <div class="ic"><span class="il">Quiz · Question 1 of 5</span><span class="id">Progress bar up top, single question per screen, gentle validation copy under the field. Shake animation instead of red errors.</span></div>

335: <div class="t"><section class="ch" id="s4">
336:   <div class="ch-num">04 — The Results Page</div>
337:   <h2>Delivering bad news<br>without losing the person.</h2>
338:   <p>The results page is the product. The challenge: show someone their score is 75/100 and have them still scroll down. Emotional arc: <strong>Anxiety → Understanding → Acceptance → Action.</strong> Every section placed with that sequence in mind.</
339:   <div class="pq"><p>Delivering bad news without making people feel hopeless — that's a product design problem, not a visual one.</p></div>
340:   <h3>The score reveal</h3>
341:   <p>Tried a full circle gauge first — confusing, no clear good/bad direction. Landed on a half-circle needle. Score counts up from 0, settles, risk badge fades in after. The animation isn't decoration. The score is what people came to see.</p>
342: </section></div>
343: 
344: <div class="w">
345: <div class="ib">
347:   <div class="ic"><span class="il">Score reveal</span><span class="id">A half-circle gauge with an animated needle. The number counts up from 0 and settles, then the risk badge fades in. The whole results page is designed to land this single moment.<
348: </div>
349: <div class="ib">
351:   <div class="ic"><span class="il">Reality check</span><span class="id">Three role-specific signals that explain why the score is what it is. Numbered for scanability and written in plain language — no jargon, no hedging.</span></div>
352: </div>
353: </div>

386: <div class="t"><section class="ch" id="s5">
387:   <div class="ch-num">05 — The Design System</div>
388:   <h2>Warm, not clinical.<br>White is the call.</h2>
390:   <p>One visual rule: warm, not clinical. Most tools in this space feel cold and threatening. We went the opposite direction.</p>
391: </section></div>
392: 
393: <div class="w">
394: <div class="d4">
395:   <div class="dc"><div class="dn" style="font-size:15px;font-family:'DM Mono',monospace;letter-spacing:.02em">#FAF9F7</div><div class="dd">Warm beige background — not stark white</div></div>
396:   <div class="dc"><div class="dn" style="font-size:15px;font-family:'DM Mono',monospace;letter-spacing:.02em">#CC3320</div><div class="dd">Red accent — urgency without alarm</div></div>
397:   <div class="dc"><div class="dn" style="font-size:14px;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:-.02em">Sora 700</div><div class="dd">Headings — geometric, tight, confident</div></div>
398:   <div class="dc"><div class="dn" style="font-size:14px;font-weight:300;letter-spacing:0">Sora 300</div><div class="dd">Body — light weight, long-form readable</div></div>
399: </div>
400: </div>
401: 
402: <div class="t">
403:   <h3>Risk color system</h3>
404:   <p>Low (0–30): green. Moderate (31–55): amber. High (56–75): coral. Critical (76–100): red. The gauge gradient, risk badge, and table rows all use the same four colors. Severity communicated visually without spelling it out.</p>
405: </div>
406: 
407: <div class="w">
408: <div class="ip">
409:   <div>
411:     <div class="ic"><span class="il">Landing · warm framing</span><span class="id">The page opens on a warm beige, not stark white. The problem is named directly in the words the user is already thinking — no marketing speak.</span></div>
412:   </div>
413:   <div>
415:     <div class="ic"><span class="il">Landing · contrast break</span><span class="id">A single dark section within the otherwise light page. Gives the long scroll a visual rhythm and pulls attention back at a deliberate moment.</span></div>
416:   </div>
417: </div>

423: <div class="t"><section class="ch" id="s6">

497: <div class="t"><section class="ch" id="s8">
498:   <div class="ch-num">08 — What We Got Wrong</div>
499:   <h2>The honest list.</h2>
500:   <ul class="ml-list">
501:     <li class="ml-item">
502:       <span class="mn">01</span>
503:       <div class="mb">
504:         <strong>Built too much before validating.</strong>
505:         <p>Full payment flow, OTP auth, dashboard, PDF download — all before a single paying user. Architecture got complex before the core value was proven. Validate first, build infrastructure second.</p>
506:       </div>
507:     </li>
508:     <li class="ml-item">
509:       <span class="mn">02</span>
510:       <div class="mb">
511:         <strong>The AI provider swap cost real time.</strong>
512:         <p>Claude → Gemini → Claude. Two codebases in parallel for a while. Commenting rather than deleting was smart — but pick a provider and don't switch without a real reason.</p>
513:       </div>
514:     </li>
515:     <li class="ml-item">
516:       <span class="mn">03</span>
517:       <div class="mb">
518:         <strong>429 error. Mid-demo. In front of users.</strong>
519:         <p>Hit Gemini's free tier quota during a testing session. Free tiers are for development. Always have billing set up before showing the product to anyone.</p>
520:       </div>
521:     </li>
522:     <li class="ml-item">
523:       <span class="mn">04</span>
524:       <div class="mb">
525:         <strong>Optimized before validating traffic.</strong>
526:         <p>Built a full architecture optimization plan — Redis caching, streaming APIs, URL-addressable reports. All good ideas. All premature. Build for the stage you're in, not the stage you want to be in.</p>
527:       </div>
528:     </li>
529:   </ul>
530:   <div class="pq"><p>Built the payment modal before activating the keys. Full auth system before a single paying user. The same mistake, twice.</p></div>

545: <div class="t"><section class="ch" id="s10">
546:   <div class="ch-num">10 — What I Learned</div>
547:   <h2>The actual takeaways.</h2>
548: 
549:   <div class="lrn">
550:     <div class="lt">Emotional products need emotional design.</div>
551:     <p>The gauge animation, the loading microcopy, the reality check opening with the user's actual job title — every decision that moved the needle was about how someone feels at a specific moment. Visual design is the surface. Emotional choreograph
552:   </div>
553:   <div class="lrn">
554:     <div class="lt">Copy is design.</div>
555:     <p>"Scanning your role…" changed user behavior more than any visual change. "One-time · No subscription · Saved to your account" answered three objections in one line. The words are the design.</p>
556:   </div>
557:   <div class="lrn">
558:     <div class="lt">Data ends arguments.</div>
559:     <p>The Baymard 26% stat ended the login-wall debate. The 6.5% churn number killed subscriptions. Data doesn't make decisions — it removes the opinion-based stalling that slows everything down.</p>
560:   </div>
561:   <div class="lrn">
562:     <div class="lt">Scope is a trap.</div>
563:     <p>Build the thing that proves the value. Everything else is a bet without the information to make it well.</p>
564:   </div>
565:   <div class="lrn">
566:     <div class="lt">Distribution is the actual battle.</div>
567:     <p>Not features, not polish. Getting found. Fifty SEO blog posts targeting "will AI replace [job] in India in 2026" — that's the unglamorous work that moves the needle. The product is ready.</p>
568:   </div>
569: 
570:   <div class="pq"><p>The best thing we built wasn't the gauge animation or the live jobs API. It was making the score feel meaningful. Everything else supports that.</p></div>
